Know this before pack one

Packs come back to you

In a four-player pod, your 14-card first pack returns with six cards after everyone has taken two.

So you will see every pack you open twice. Remember what you passed, but don't build around it coming back: three other people each take two.

You are not locked into the colors of your first pick.

Echoed pairs

Every Play Booster has three bonus-sheet legends: one true echoed pair and one unpaired legend.

  • Before each game, you may reveal two different cards from one echoed pair. They start the game in exile.
  • You may cast one of them as though it were in your hand. Then you may shuffle the other into your library.
  • One pair per game. Two copies of the same card are not a pair. Normal costs and timing apply.
  • You don't have to draft a pair. A pair is not a reason to force a third color.

Draft winner gets a Reality Fracture set symbol pin, while supplies last.

The set's main character

Your Jace token

  • −1: surveil 1. −3: draw a card.
  • One ability per turn, in your main phase. Adding loyalty doesn't give you a second use.
  • It's a planeswalker, not a creature. It can't block, and your opponent can attack it.
  • Protect the board before chasing value. A Jace that dies to one attack gives you less than a plain blocker.

When the pack hits you

The ten-second pick check

  1. Can I play early? Check your two- and three-drops before taking another expensive reward.
  2. What enables this? Name the card that makes the payoff work. A second payoff isn't an enabler.
  3. What answers their threat? Keep removal. Check whether it hits planeswalkers too.
  4. Can I use both picks? Two cards that look like a pair can want different decks.

mtg.guide, Reality Fracture start page

Early, middle, late

Early picksExplore. Take strong cards you can cast without a hard mana commitment. Notice a pattern of good cards in one color, not one surprise.
Middle picksMake it work. A plain creature that fills your empty two-mana slot can beat another five-drop. Signals are evidence, not a promise.
Late picksFill holes. Take cards that make your deck over cards that only stop someone else. You may never play that person.

Wizards' deckbuilding order

Build from threats, then removal

  1. Sort by color. Put multicolor, artifacts, and lands in their own piles.
  2. Find your threats, the cards that can win on their own. One or two in a color is a reason to play it.
  3. Add removal. Counterspells and discard count.
  4. Pick your two best colors. Three colors stretches your mana and can lock you out of games.
  5. Say how the deck wins in one sentence. Cut what doesn't help that sentence.

Rules for every deck

  • A prepared spell still costs mana. It's only there while the creature survives.
  • Jace is a choice, not free cards. Surveil, draw, and granted abilities share one activation.
  • Hybrid cards fit either color. A W/U hybrid doesn't need both.
  • Fixing has to make the right color. Heartwoods only make red or green.
  • A playable plus fixing beats two exciting cards you can't cast.

mtg.guide, Build: rules for every deck

At the table

  • Keep hands with a route into the game. Lands plus a play by turn 2 or 3 beats lands plus bombs.
  • Attacking Jace or the player? Pick the one that stops them winning. Don't send everything at Jace if you have lethal.
  • Use removal on real problems. "Always save it for a bomb" is too rigid.
  • Announce clearly: loyalty abilities, prepared spells, and who or what you're attacking.
  • Not sure how two cards interact? Ask. This is a casual event.
